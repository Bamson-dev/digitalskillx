AI Ads Automation Masterclass: assessment structure

Design principle: students must show they can plan and launch a campaign. Quizzes check understanding. Assignments check practice. The final project checks the whole process.

1. Quizzes
Five quizzes, six questions each (five multiple choice, one short answer). Pass mark 70%. Unlimited retries are suggested so students learn from explanations. Short answers need manual or rubric based marking in the LMS.

Quiz 1: Objectives and conversion tracking (placed after Module 4)

Q1. You sell a ₦14,999 online course and your payment confirmation page can send a Purchase event. Which optimisation event is the best starting choice?
   A. Link click
   B. Purchase, or an earlier event if you cannot get enough Purchase events
   C. Page like
   D. 3 second video view
   Correct answer: B
   Explanation: Meta learns from the event you choose. Choose the one closest to the real result you want, and step back to an earlier event only when volume is too low.

Q2. At which level do you choose the image, video and text a person sees?
   A. Campaign
   B. Ad set
   C. Ad
   D. Business portfolio
   Correct answer: C
   Explanation: The ad level holds the creative, copy and destination.

Q3. Why test your tracking events before spending real budget?
   A. Meta charges for untested events
   B. To confirm each event fires once, with the right value and currency, so Meta learns from correct data
   C. Testing improves your Page rating
   D. It is only needed for video ads
   Correct answer: B
   Explanation: Wrong or duplicated events teach the system the wrong thing.

Q4. A campaign optimised for link clicks brings many visits and no buyers. What is the most likely reason?
   A. Meta is hiding your ad
   B. The system found people likely to click, not people likely to buy
   C. The budget is too large
   D. Instagram does not work in Nigeria
   Correct answer: B
   Explanation: The system optimises toward the event you chose.

Q5. The browser pixel and a server event both report the same purchase. What lets Meta count it once?
   A. The same event ID on both
   B. The same ad name
   C. The same Page name
   D. The same budget
   Correct answer: A
   Explanation: A shared event ID is the deduplication key.

Q6. A Lagos salon takes bookings through WhatsApp. Choose an objective and a destination, and give two sentences of reasoning.
   Short answer. Marking guide: Accept a messages or leads objective with a WhatsApp destination, or a clear alternative with sound reasoning tied to how the business receives bookings. Look for a reason that links the objective to the customer action.

Quiz 2: Research, angles, copy and creatives (placed after Module 8)

Q1. You generated 30 hooks with AI. What is the best next step?
   A. Publish all 30
   B. Select a few using written criteria, then check claims and policy
   C. Use the first one
   D. Ask AI to choose and publish automatically
   Correct answer: B
   Explanation: AI generates options. You choose and verify.

Q2. An AI draft says 'Guaranteed to double your sales.' What should you do?
   A. Keep it because it is persuasive
   B. Remove it, because you cannot prove it and it can break advertising rules
   C. Move it to the last line
   D. Add an asterisk
   Correct answer: B
   Explanation: Do not promise results you cannot prove.

Q3. Which proof can you use in an ad?
   A. Proof AI invented to fit the angle
   B. A real customer message you have permission to use
   C. A competitor's testimonial
   D. A made up statistic that sounds right
   Correct answer: B
   Explanation: Use only proof you actually have.

Q4. Your AI generated product image shows a distorted label. What do you do?
   A. Publish it, nobody will notice
   B. Fix it, regenerate it, or use a real photo
   C. Add more text over it
   D. Run it as a test
   Correct answer: B
   Explanation: Check every AI image before it goes live.

Q5. Your audience does not yet know they have the problem. What should the opening do?
   A. Describe your product features
   B. Describe a situation they recognise from their own day
   C. State a discount
   D. Show your logo
   Correct answer: B
   Explanation: Unaware audiences respond to recognition, not product detail.

Q6. Edit this ad so it has no exaggerated claims and no hype: 'Our amazing course will make you rich in 7 days! Guaranteed results! Click now!!!' Rewrite it in two or three sentences about a course that teaches AI ads, price ₦14,999.
   Short answer. Marking guide: Look for removal of guarantees and income promises, no stacked exclamation marks, a specific description of what the course teaches, the price stated honestly, and a calm call to action.

Quiz 3: Landing pages and tracking (placed after Module 10)

Q1. What does the Test Events tool let you do?
   A. See events from your browser arriving in real time
   B. Change your ad budget
   C. Edit your Page
   D. Delete old events
   Correct answer: A
   Explanation: It is the fastest way to confirm an event reaches Meta.

Q2. What value should a Purchase event carry?
   A. A guess of the product value
   B. The real amount paid, with currency NGN
   C. Always 1
   D. The ad budget
   Correct answer: B
   Explanation: Value and currency should match the actual payment.

Q3. Your thank-you page fires Purchase again each time it is refreshed. What is the risk?
   A. None
   B. Purchases are counted more than once, which distorts your numbers
   C. The page will load faster
   D. Meta will reject the pixel
   Correct answer: B
   Explanation: Fire the event once per payment reference.

Q4. If you send customer email to Meta through server events, how must it be sent?
   A. In plain text
   B. Normalised and hashed, never plain text
   C. Only in the ad name
   D. Not at all, ever
   Correct answer: B
   Explanation: Hash customer identifiers before sending.

Q5. Your page takes about nine seconds to load on mobile data. What is the likely effect?
   A. More buyers
   B. Many visitors leave before the page loads
   C. Lower CPM
   D. No effect
   Correct answer: B
   Explanation: Compress images and simplify the page.

Q6. List the events your funnel should send, in order, from ad click to payment, and say where each fires.
   Short answer. Marking guide: Expect PageView, ViewContent, Lead or InitiateCheckout where relevant, and Purchase on a confirmed payment page. Each event should have a clear location in the funnel.

Quiz 4: Budgets, tests and metrics (placed after Module 13)

Q1. Click through rate is best described as:
   A. Clicks divided by impressions
   B. Spend divided by purchases
   C. Reach divided by budget
   D. Purchases divided by clicks
   Correct answer: A
   Explanation: It shows how often people who saw the ad clicked.

Q2. CPM measures:
   A. Cost per 1,000 impressions
   B. Cost per message
   C. Clicks per minute
   D. Customers per month
   Correct answer: A
   Explanation: CPM is the cost of 1,000 impressions.

Q3. CPC is high and CTR is low. Where should you look first?
   A. Your payment page
   B. The hook, creative or audience
   C. Your invoice
   D. Your Instagram bio
   Correct answer: B
   Explanation: Low CTR points to the ad itself or who sees it.

Q4. Spend is ₦30,000 and you recorded 10 sales at ₦14,999 each. What is the cost per purchase?
   A. ₦1,500
   B. ₦3,000
   C. ₦14,999
   D. ₦30,000
   Correct answer: B
   Explanation: ₦30,000 divided by 10 equals ₦3,000.

Q5. With the same figures, what is the return on ad spend before other costs?
   A. About 0.5
   B. About 2
   C. About 5
   D. About 15
   Correct answer: C
   Explanation: Revenue ₦149,990 divided by spend ₦30,000 is about 5.0. This ignores product and other costs.

Q6. Frequency is rising and results are getting worse over several days. Give two possible causes and one test for each.
   Short answer. Marking guide: Accept creative fatigue and audience saturation or similar, each paired with a specific, single variable test such as a new creative or a wider audience.

Quiz 5: Diagnosis, scaling and compliance (placed after Module 15)

Q1. Which set of signals best supports a decision to scale?
   A. One good day
   B. Several stable days, positive unit economics and verified tracking
   C. A high CTR alone
   D. A friend's comment
   Correct answer: B
   Explanation: Scale on evidence, not on one result.

Q2. Which approach is safer when increasing budget?
   A. Gradual increases with a stop rule
   B. Tripling the budget overnight
   C. Changing everything at once
   D. Deleting the ad set
   Correct answer: A
   Explanation: Large jumps can disturb delivery. Increase in steps and record each one.

Q3. An ad is rejected. What is the best first step?
   A. Create ten copies
   B. Read the reason, compare it with the current policy, then edit or appeal
   C. Ignore it
   D. Switch ad accounts
   Correct answer: B
   Explanation: Understand the reason before acting.

Q4. Which item must you never paste into an AI assistant?
   A. A draft hook
   B. Access tokens, passwords or private customer data
   C. A product description
   D. A public competitor ad
   Correct answer: B
   Explanation: Keep secrets and private data out of AI tools.

Q5. You duplicate an ad set to test a new audience. What is one risk?
   A. Audience overlap and a split budget
   B. Lower image quality
   C. Page name changes
   D. Nothing
   Correct answer: A
   Explanation: Overlapping audiences can compete and split spend.

Q6. Cost per purchase has risen for three days while frequency has grown. List your diagnosis steps in order.
   Short answer. Marking guide: Expect a structured path: confirm tracking, check delivery and frequency, check creative fatigue, check landing page and offer, then choose the smallest single change to retest.

2. Lesson assignments
Each lesson has one assignment, listed below. Suggested LMS setup: create an assignment only for the lessons marked Required. Other lessons keep the task as an optional exercise inside the lesson text.

1.1 (Required): Write your business in four lines: what you sell, price in Naira, who buys, and how a customer pays you today.
1.2 (Optional): Complete the business profile prompt from the Prompt Library and save the AI output in your campaign file.
2.1 (Optional): Draw the campaign structure for your own offer with one campaign, two ad sets and three ads.
2.2 (Optional): List the three formats you will test first and write one sentence on why each suits your customer.
3.1 (Optional): Screenshot your ad account overview page (hide sensitive details) showing the account is active with a payment method added.
3.2 (Required): Read the current policy page for your category and write three things your ads must not say.
4.1 (Optional): Write your goal, your chosen objective, your conversion event and the destination, with one sentence of reasoning.
4.2 (Required): List which events your funnel should send, in order, from ad click to payment.
5.1 (Optional): Run the research brief prompt for your business and mark each claim as verified, to verify, or discard.
5.2 (Optional): Collect 20 real customer phrases from public sources and cluster them into pains, desires and objections.
5.3 (Required): Write three audience hypotheses and the Ads Manager settings that match each.
6.1 (Optional): Create an angle map with at least six angles for your offer.
6.2 (Required): Submit your scoring table and the three angles you chose with one sentence each on why.
7.1 (Optional): Produce 30 hooks for your top angle and mark the best five with your reasons.
7.2 (Optional): Write three full ads for your best angle, one per awareness level.
7.3 (Required): Edit your three ads with the checklist and submit before and after versions.
8.1 (Optional): Complete a creative brief for each of your three chosen angles.
8.2 (Optional): Create three static ads and submit the exported files with the brief each belongs to.
8.3 (Required): Produce one short video ad under 30 seconds with captions and submit it with the script.
9.1 (Optional): Sketch your landing page section by section and write the headline that matches your best ad.
9.2 (Optional): Publish your page and submit the link plus a screenshot of a successful test on a phone.
9.3 (Required): Write your first three WhatsApp replies and decide whether your funnel uses WhatsApp, a page, or both.
10.1 (Optional): Submit a screenshot of your pixel receiving a PageView from your page.
10.2 (Optional): Decide whether you need server events now, and list who will set them up.
10.3 (Required): Submit your tracking test log showing each event, its value and pass or fail.
11.1 (Optional): Create the naming table and UTM links for your campaign.
11.2 (Optional): Build your campaign in draft and submit screenshots of each level.
11.3 (Required): Create a reusable weekly report prompt and test it on sample data from the tracker.
12.1 (Optional): Calculate your break even cost per customer and your test budget, and show your working.
12.2 (Optional): Write your test plan: question, variable, ads, budget, duration and decision rule.
12.3 (Required): Write five decision rules for your campaign and apply them to the sample data provided.
13.1 (Optional): Build a custom column set and explain in one line what each column tells you.
13.2 (Required): Complete a weekly review on the sample data and submit your three decisions.
14.1 (Optional): Diagnose three symptom cases and name the first thing you would check for each.
14.2 (Required): Submit a diagnosis and fix plan for the two sample datasets.
15.1 (Optional): Complete the scaling readiness checklist for your campaign and state yes or not yet with reasons.
15.2 (Optional): Write your scaling plan with limits and a stop rule.
15.3 (Required): Write a response plan for a rejected ad and list the proof you keep for your claims.
16.1 (Required): Submit Stage 1: the complete Campaign Planning Worksheet for your campaign.
16.2 (Required): Submit Stage 2: screenshots of every level and the completed launch checklist.
16.3 (Required): Submit Stage 3: your seven day results report and next actions. If your budget does not allow a live run, submit a draft campaign with a full test plan.

Assignment grading, applied to every required assignment: Complete (the task is done and uses the student's own business or a clearly labelled hypothetical), Reasoned (the choices are explained in plain words), Accurate (arithmetic and Meta terms are correct), Honest (no invented results, customers or screenshots). Grade as Pass, Revise or Fail. Give written feedback on Revise.

3. Final campaign project (Module 16)
Students may run a live campaign with their own budget or prepare a complete draft campaign if budget does not allow it. Results are not graded. Reasoning and process are.

Stages: Stage 1 Plan (worksheet, audience research, angle scoring). Stage 2 Launch review (screenshots of every level, completed launch checklist, tracking test log). Stage 3 Results report (seven day report, weekly review, three decisions and next actions).

Rubric, 100 points, pass mark 70:
  Planning and numbers: 20 points
  Research and angles: 15 points
  Copy and creative: 20 points
  Landing page and tracking: 15 points
  Campaign build and launch review: 15 points
  Review and optimisation: 15 points

Not accepted: invented results, invented customers or fake screenshots; claims beyond the student's own data; copied competitor wording.

The full rubric with descriptions of strong work is in the Campaign Project Brief and Rubric PDF (resource 09).

4. Suggested completion rules
Course completion percentage: 100% of lessons viewed. Certificate: all five quizzes at 70% or better and the final project passed at 70 points or better. Final project marking is manual by the instructor.

5. Grading workload for the instructor
Required assignments: 17 per student. Final project: 3 submissions per student. Decide before launch who marks these and the turnaround time you promise students. Without a marker, convert required assignments to optional self checks and keep only the final project as a marked item.
