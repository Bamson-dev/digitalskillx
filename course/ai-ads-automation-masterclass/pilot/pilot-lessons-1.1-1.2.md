AI Ads Automation Masterclass: two lesson pilot package (lessons 1.1 and 1.2)

Purpose
Record two real lessons, publish them to the Draft course through the real upload path, and test them as a student before you record the other 39. The pilot answers the open questions: does the video play, do attachments download for a student, does progress save, and how long does recording and editing take you.

Rules for both lessons
No personal stories, case studies, results or statistics appear in the scripts. Every example is labelled as invented. Do not read prices or policy details from memory. Do not show the AI tool brand on screen if you decided not to show it (decision D1 in the decision report). Use plain English, full sentences, no pidgin, no em dashes, no emojis.

The objectives and structure below are the existing ones from the curriculum. They are unchanged.

RECORDING SCRIPT (read at about 140 words per minute, with pauses on each slide)

[Slide 1]
Welcome to the AI Ads Automation Masterclass. This is lesson 1.1. In this lesson you will learn what AI does well in Facebook and Instagram advertising, where it fails, and which decisions you keep for yourself.

[Slide 2]
By the end, you can explain which parts of campaign work AI speeds up and which decisions stay with you. That one skill saves you money, because most wasted ad spend starts with a decision somebody handed to a tool.

[Slide 3]
Let us start with the workflow. Every Meta campaign moves through the same stages. You research your customer. You find angles, which are the reasons someone would care about your offer. You write the copy. You prepare the creative, which means the image or video. You build the campaign in Ads Manager. You launch it. And you review what happened.

AI can touch every one of these stages. It does not do every stage equally well, so you need to know where to trust it and where to check it.

[Slide 4]
First, where AI helps. AI is good at four things.

It summarises research quickly. If you give it text about your customers, it can sort that text into themes in seconds.

It writes first drafts. You get a starting point in minutes instead of staring at a blank page.

It produces variations. You can ask for ten ways to say the same thing and pick the three worth testing.

And it summarises reports. If you paste in your own numbers, it can describe what changed.

In every one of these, you give AI material and it speeds up the work. It does not replace your judgment.

[Slide 5]
Now, where AI fails. There are four problems to watch for.

AI invents facts. It can state a delivery time, a feature or a number that is not true for your business. It says it confidently, so you must check every claim.

AI gets the tone wrong. It often writes in a style that sounds like an advertisement written by a machine. Your customers will notice.

AI has policy blind spots. It does not know whether a sentence will pass Meta's advertising policies, and those policies change. You check the current policy yourself before you launch.

And AI has no access to your real account numbers. Unless you paste them in, it does not know your spend, your cost per result or your sales. If it gives you numbers you did not provide, they are guesses.

[Slide 6]
Here is an example of the difference. This is an invented ad for a hypothetical catering business in Lagos. It is not a real business and not a real result.

On the left is a raw AI draft. It says "Amazing catering in Lagos! Best jollof ever! Guaranteed to impress all your guests! Order now and thank us later!"

Look at what is wrong. "Best" and "guaranteed" are claims nobody can prove. "Amazing" says nothing. There is no price, no guest count and no clear next step.

On the right is the human edited version. "Catering for parties of 30 to 200 guests in Lagos. Choose your menu, confirm your date, pay a deposit to book. Send us your date and guest count on WhatsApp for a quote."

This version says who it is for, what happens next and how to act. The AI produced the starting point. The human made it usable. That is how you will work throughout this course.

[Slide 7]
So here is the rule for the whole course. AI drafts. You decide. The data judges.

AI drafts means you let it do the first pass. You decide means you edit, you reject and you approve. The data judges means that after the campaign runs, the numbers from your own account decide whether an ad was good. Not your opinion and not the AI's opinion.

[Slide 8]
One more point before your assignment. Results do not come from a tool alone. They depend on your offer, your price, your audience, your creative, your tracking and your budget. If your offer is unclear or your price does not match what customers can pay, no AI tool fixes that. This course teaches you to use AI inside a sound process. It does not promise any result, because results depend on all six of these things, and some of them are outside any tool.

[Slide 9]
Your assignment is short. Write your business in four lines. Line one: what you sell. Line two: the price in Naira. Line three: who buys it. Line four: how a customer pays you today. Use the Campaign Planning Worksheet from the lesson resources, or any document. You will use these four lines again in lesson 1.2.

[Slide 10]
In the next lesson you will set up your folder, your naming system and your AI workspace. See you there.

Script length: about 830 spoken words. At 140 words per minute that is about 6 minutes, or 7 to 8 with pauses on the slides. The curriculum plans 14 minutes. This is a real gap, and it probably applies to other lessons: the planned minutes are estimates, not measurements. Do not pad the lesson. Record it, note the real length, and set the lesson duration in the LMS to the real figure. Then update lessons.csv, so the course total reflects measured minutes. If every lesson runs about half its planned length, the recording workload in production-plan.md drops by about the same ratio.

Student assignment: complete the business profile prompt from the Prompt Library and save the AI output in your campaign file.

SLIDE OUTLINE (4 slides, shown at the start and end)
Slide 1. Title: Your course workflow and campaign file. Lesson 1.2.
Slide 2. What you will set up: a campaign folder, a naming system, an AI workspace with a saved business profile.
Slide 3. Naming pattern: one line per level. Campaign, ad set, ad. Example names below, labelled invented.

SCREEN RECORDING INSTRUCTIONS
Set up before recording: close other windows and notifications, set the screen to 1920 by 1080, set the browser zoom to 125 percent so text is readable on a phone, hide bookmarks, use a clean browser profile. Make a fresh AI chat so no earlier history shows. Use a dummy business, not a real customer. Do not show passwords, tokens or private customer data. Do not enter real client information.
Part 1 (about 3 minutes): in your file manager, create a folder named "Campaigns". Inside it, create one folder named with the naming pattern. Open the Campaign Planning Worksheet and save a copy in that folder.
Part 2 (about 5 minutes): open your AI assistant. Open the Prompt Library PDF at section A, prompt A1. Replace each bracketed item with details for an invented demo business. Paste the prompt. Show the AI reply. Read the five line summary. Correct one wrong detail and show how you correct it. Paste prompt A2 (banned phrases and rules) and show it confirming the rules.
Part 3 (about 3 minutes): copy the AI output into the "Business profile" section of your campaign file. Show how to pin or save the chat so you can reuse it. Show a list of prompt names in a notes file called "Prompts that worked".
Part 4 (about 1 minute): return to slides and explain how the final project uses the same folder.

DEMO BUSINESS (invented, for recording only)
I run a business in Lagos, Nigeria. I sell a one day weekend phone photography class for beginners for [PRICE IN NAIRA, choose a round number you are comfortable showing]. My customers are adults who want better photos of their families and small businesses. They pay by bank transfer. My goal for the next 30 days is to fill one class of 20 seats. My tone is friendly and direct.

RECORDING SCRIPT

[Slide 1]
Welcome to lesson 1.2. In this lesson you set up the three things you will use for the rest of the course: a campaign folder, a naming system and an AI workspace that already knows your business.

[Slide 2]
We will do this on screen so you can follow each click. First the folder, then the names, then your AI assistant.

[Slide 3, then switch to screen]
A quick word on naming. Ad accounts get messy fast. Three weeks from now you will not remember which ad was which. So use a pattern. For each level, write the same parts in the same order.

For a campaign, use the date, the offer, and the objective. For an ad set, add the audience. For an ad, add the angle and the version number. Here is an invented example. Campaign: "2611 Photo class Sales". Ad set: "2611 Photo class Sales Parents Lagos". Ad: "2611 Photo class Sales Parents Lagos Family photos v1".

The exact words matter less than being consistent. Pick a pattern and keep it.

[Screen, Part 1]
Now to the screen. I am creating a folder called Campaigns. Inside it I create one folder for this campaign, using the pattern. Every file for this campaign lives here: your research, your copy, your screenshots, your tracker and your notes. One campaign, one folder.

I open the Campaign Planning Worksheet from the lesson resources, and I save a copy into this folder. This is your campaign file. You will fill it in as the course goes on.

[Screen, Part 2]
Next, the AI assistant. You can use any assistant you already have. A free account is enough to start. I am opening a new chat so you do not see old messages.

Open the Prompt Library, section A, prompt A1. This is the business profile prompt. You replace everything in brackets with your real details. For this demonstration I am using an invented business, a weekend phone photography class in Lagos. It is not a real business.

I paste the prompt. Notice that the prompt tells the AI not to invent facts and to ask me when it is unsure. It also asks the AI to summarise the profile back to me in five lines. This step matters. It shows you whether the AI understood you.

Here is the reply. I read it. [Point to one wrong or vague detail in the reply.] This line is not quite right, so I correct it in the chat. Always check this summary. If the profile is wrong, everything the AI writes afterwards starts from a wrong base.

Now prompt A2. This sets the writing rules: no income promises, no guaranteed results, no exaggerated claims, no fake testimonials. The AI lists the rules back, which confirms it has them.

[Screen, Part 3]
I copy the corrected profile into the Business profile section of my campaign file. Then I save the chat so I can return to it. In a notes file called Prompts that worked, I write the prompt name, the date, and one line on what it produced. When a prompt works well, you keep it. When it fails, you write down why.

[Slide 4]
This file matters later. In the final project you submit three stages: your plan, your launch review and your results report. All three come from the folder you just made.

Your assignment is to do this for your own business. Complete the business profile prompt from the Prompt Library, check the AI summary, correct it, and save the output in your campaign file. Do not paste passwords, access tokens or private customer data into any AI tool.

See you in lesson 2.1.
